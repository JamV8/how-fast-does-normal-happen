A visual essay on the Central Limit Theorem

How fast does
normal happen?

Statistics runs on one promise: average enough data and you get a bell curve. Textbooks say thirty observations is enough. I tested it.

Scroll
01

Every dataset has a shape.

These are 1,100 waiting times. Most are short. A few are very long, which stretches the shape to the right.

02

Take a sample. Average it.

Pick five at random and collapse them into a single number: their average.

03

Now repeat it, thousands of times.

The averages pile up into a shape of their own. With samples of five, it still leans to one side.

04

Make every sample bigger.

Keep scrolling. As each sample grows, the averages tighten and straighten toward the bell curve.

At n = 30 it still leans. At n = 71 it passes all four of my normality tests.

05

The answer depends on the data.

Same test, ten populations. Symmetric data settle in a handful of observations. Real-world prices, delays and populations need hundreds, or thousands.

06

Some data never settle.

The Cauchy distribution produces extreme values so often that averaging never tames it. Watch the sample size climb to 5,000. The shape does not move.

07

So, is it thirty?

No.

It can be 8. It can be 71. It can be 2,450. And for some data, no number works.
